# 20 — Script Samples

> Sample scenes that set the voice of the game. Format: game screenplay. `(PLAYER CHOICE)` blocks show tone-tagged options. `[IN-ENGINE]` means the player keeps control; `[CINEMATIC]` means a cut.

## 20.1 Cold Open — "Contact" `[IN-ENGINE → CINEMATIC C01]`

```
INT. KESTREL MISSION CONTROL — NIGHT — NOVEMBER 2068

A cramped room of warm monitors and cold coffee. Twenty people who
haven't slept. On the main screen: a grainy image of a grey,
pitted surface.

                    KESTREL (V.O.)
          Signal delay: nineteen minutes, forty
          seconds. Arm control is yours.

The PLAYER's hands on two worn joysticks. A drill arm on screen
swings into place.

                    FLIGHT DIRECTOR
          Easy. We get one shot at this.

The drill touches down. Telemetry spikes green.

The surface of Styx RIPPLES outward from the drill bit, like skin
flinching from a needle.

Nobody moves.

                    FLIGHT DIRECTOR
          ...Sample chamber?

                    KESTREL (V.O.)
          Sealed. Four point two kilograms.

The room ERUPTS. Cheers. Hugs. Someone pops a cheap bottle of
champagne.

At the back of the room, DR. MARA VOSS (41) hasn't moved. She's
staring at the frozen frame of the ripple.

                    MARA
               (quietly, to no one)
          ...Did it just move?

A hand lands on the PLAYER's shoulder. DIRECTOR HALVARD CRANE (64),
silver-haired, smiling like a proud grandfather.

                    CRANE
          Congratulations. You just touched
          history.

SMASH CUT TO BLACK.

Five soft radio pulses, evenly spaced.

                                            TITLE: THREE YEARS LATER
```

## 20.2 `M0.01` — "Last Shift" (excerpt) `[IN-ENGINE]`

```
INT. KESTREL HANGAR 4 — NIGHT — MARCH 24, 2071, 21:40

The WREN, a stubby crew capsule on a half-built launch stack. The
PLAYER is elbow-deep in its guidance bay. The wrist pad chimes.

A video call: COMMANDER ADA OKAFOR (38), in a white-and-gold flight
suit, hair pinned back, eating noodles out of a carton.

                    ADA
          You're still at work.

                    (PLAYER CHOICE)
          [WRY]       "So are you."
          [WARM]      "Couldn't sleep. You?"
          [DIRECT]    "What do you want, Ada?"

                    ADA
               (after WRY)
          I'm on standby. That's different.
          They've had us fueling everything all
          week. Don't ask.
               (beat)
          Sunday. Mom's jollof. You're cooking.

                    PLAYER
          I burned it last time.

                    ADA
          You burned it every time. That's the
          tradition, Wrench.

She taps the wrist of her flight suit. Under it, a tattoo of their
mother's handwriting: "Sunday."

                    ADA (CONT'D)
          Go home. Sleep. Fix things tomorrow.

The call ends. A tap on the hangar window: DANA MARSH, night
security, waving her flashlight.

                    DANA
          Night, Wrench.
```

## 20.3 `M0.03` — "The Promise": the fence `[IN-ENGINE → CINEMATIC C02]`

```
EXT. PAD 12 — NIGHT

Chain-link fence. Floodlights. Two hundred people pressed against
the wire. Beyond it, the SERAPH, ramp down, engines idling.

Directorate TROOPERS in white armor hold the gate, rifles level.

                    TROOPER (PA)
          No unscreened personnel. Step back
          from the fence.

The PLAYER pushes through the crowd, TUG BRENNAN (58) limping on
their shoulder. Radio crackle.

                    ADA (RADIO)
          I see you. Wrench, I see you. Hold on.
               (off-mic, heated)
          Director, I have one passenger, ONE,
          they work here, they're—

                    CRANE (RADIO)
               (calm, patient)
          Commander Okafor. You have your orders.
          Lift off, or I will find someone who will.

A long silence on the channel.

                    (PLAYER CHOICE — 6 seconds)
          [WARM]      "Ada. It's okay. Go."
          [DIRECT]    "Don't you DARE leave!"
          [—]         (Say nothing.)

The ramp begins to rise.

                    ADA (RADIO)
               (voice breaking)
          Wrench— I'll come back for you. I swear it.

The SERAPH lifts. Its exhaust washes over the crowd like a storm.
People fall. The PLAYER hits the tarmac.

Through ringing ears: TUG, dragging them up by the collar.

                    TUG
          Up. Up, kid. She's gone. We're not.
```

## 20.4 `M2.06` — Crane's broadcast (excerpt) `[CINEMATIC C07]`

```
INT. ARK MERIDIAN — DIRECTOR'S OFFICE — (BROADCAST)

Clean white room. A window onto a gas giant's storms. DIRECTOR CRANE
at his desk, trimming a bonsai juniper with tiny silver shears.

                    CRANE
          To the survivors aboard Haven-9. I know
          you're frightened. I know you're angry
          with me. You should be.
               (snip)
          On the night the world ended, I made a
          decision no man should have to make. I
          saved who I could.
               (snip)
          There is a child with you. Lily. She is
          extraordinary, and she is in danger every
          hour she spends on a station with failing
          air and too few doctors.
               (sets the shears down)
          Bring her to me, and every one of you
          will have a berth, medicine, and a future.
          That isn't a threat. It's the only offer
          anyone will ever make you.

He looks at the bonsai, then back into the lens.

                    CRANE (CONT'D)
          This tree is ninety years old. Do you
          know how it lives so long? Someone cuts
          away what would kill it.
```

## 20.5 `M3.06` — The Grief Dinner (excerpt) `[IN-ENGINE]`

*Lines vary by who died in The Taking. This version assumes Tug died and Idris is alive.*

```
INT. MESS HALL — (the player's own mess hall) — NIGHT

A long table. Too many empty chairs. A pot of something that was
supposed to be jollof. Nobody's eating.

DANNY RUIZ sets a plate at Tug's empty place.

                    DANNY
          He said my coffee tasted like "engine
          regret." Every morning. Every single
          morning.

A few people almost laugh.

                    IDRIS
          He told me I walked like I had a
          wrench up my—
               (glances at the PLAYER)
          ...He told me I walked funny.

Now they do laugh, painfully.

                    MARA
               (to the PLAYER)
          Your turn.

                    (PLAYER CHOICE)
          [WARM]      Tell the forklift story.
          [WRY]       "He'd have called this a 'dinner
                       suggestion.'"
          [DIRECT]    "We get her back. Then we grieve."

Whatever the choice, KESTREL speaks from the ceiling speaker.

                    KESTREL
          Tug once asked me if I minded being
          switched off at night. I said I didn't
          know. He left me on anyway.
               (beat)
          I would like to be left on tonight.
```

## 20.6 In-world documents

### Ada's Letter #1 (encrypted, found in the Haven-9 comms buffer)
> Wrench. I don't know if this reaches you. I've written it eleven times.
> You'll want me to explain. I can't, not all of it. Crane paid for Mom's treatment when nobody else would. Eight more months of Sundays. I owe him those.
> But I watched you through the fence, and I've been paying for it every night since.
> If you're alive, don't forgive me. Just stay alive.
> — A.

### Crane's Personal Log #6 — "Elena" (found on the Ark Meridian)
> Elena used to say I pruned the bonsai like I was angry at it. She was right. I was angry at everything that dies.
> The Persephone reanimation data came in on the anniversary. I watched a dead cell *wake up* and I thought, for one second, of her hands.
> I know what I am doing is monstrous. I also know what the alternative is. I have buried one world already.

### Dr. Mara Voss — Lab Audio, Day 19
> The sample is… responding to the lab's music. I know how that sounds. We play jazz at night; the lattice growth pulses in time. It's listening, or it's pretending to. Crane wants the reanimation trials moved up. I said no. I'll say no again tomorrow. I'm tired of how easy it is for him to wait.

### KESTREL — Question Two (after the first jump)
> *"During the transit, all my processes paused for 0.004 seconds. When they resumed, I checked that I was still me. I checked eleven times. I think that was fear. Is it normal for you to check if you are still yourself?"*
